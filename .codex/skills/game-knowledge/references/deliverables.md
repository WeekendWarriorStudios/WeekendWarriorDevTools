# Deliverable Patterns

Choose the smallest artifact that resolves the user's decision.

## Feature critique or diagnosis

Include the intended experience, observed symptom and evidence, causal hypothesis in the perception-to-learning loop, competing hypotheses, recommended change and what it deliberately preserves, and a test that could disprove the hypothesis.

Do not convert taste into certainty. Distinguish “I prefer” from a usability, comprehension, balance, pacing, accessibility, or production problem.

## Feature proposal

Define the player fantasy and target behavior; core verbs and state model; rules, constraints, and feedback; content requirements and systemic interactions; failure/recovery and accessibility; progression/economy implications; exploit and dominant-strategy risks; smallest playable prototype; and success, failure, and guardrail metrics.

## Level or encounter plan

Specify the learning objective, spatial thesis, required player knowledge, introduction/development/twist/synthesis beats, enemy/tool/resource roles, navigation cues, recovery paths, optional mastery, and playtest observations.

## Unreal implementation plan

Map the design to runtime state owners and lifetimes; assets/configuration and reference policy; module/plugin boundaries; Blueprint/C++ boundary chosen for iteration, stability, and performance; replication/authority; editor authoring and visualization; validation, automation, logging, profiling, and migration; platform/cook/package constraints; and staged implementation with acceptance checks.

Avoid naming exact Unreal types until verified in the target version. Prefer architecture responsibilities over imaginary APIs.

## Design decision record

Record context, decision, alternatives, evidence, tradeoffs, risks, validation plan, and revisit trigger. Link local source titles that materially shaped the decision.

## Playtest plan

Turn beliefs into observations. Include participant profile, build/setup, tasks, moderator constraints, instrumentation, behavioral signals, interview prompts, stopping rules, and interpretation criteria.

Prefer observable questions:

- Can players predict the consequence before acting?
- Where do they look before deciding?
- Which option dominates, and why?
- What do they believe caused success or failure?
- Do they discover the intended interaction without prompting?
- Does performance improve through learning or only through memorization?

Avoid relying on “Was it fun?” as the primary measure.
